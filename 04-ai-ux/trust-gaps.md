## 1. Black-box gap, what changed

Every risk card now renders its reasoning inline, not behind a modal. Each of the top-3 risks carries a two-part citation footer — a clickable strategy-clause link **and** an evidence link (Slack permalink / Jira ticket ID) — plus a per-risk confidence meter (0–100). The PM can verify *why* a risk is ranked where it is in under three seconds: glance at the confidence meter, click either citation to jump straight to the source. No ranking exists on the card without both halves of its citation pair showing.

---

## 2. Hallucination gap, what changed

Juno signals uncertainty instead of hiding it. Any risk below 70% confidence is flagged "review before acting" with its meter shown in amber; when fewer than 3 relevant chunks are retrieved, Juno withholds the ranking entirely and shows an "Insufficient evidence to recommend priority" banner rather than guessing. When Juno *is* wrong, the PM edits just the wrong part — the Override button lets them re-tag P0–P3 or correct either half of the citation pair (strategy clause **or** evidence) inline, without rewriting the whole card from scratch.

---

## 3. Control gap, what changed

Every card carries Approve / Override / Escalate buttons, and the kill switch covers every ranking Juno produces — confidence ≥70% or not. Juno is draft-only: it never auto-sends and posts only to #pm-daily / #pm-juno-review, and because V1 is read-only, nothing lands in Notion or Jira until the PM copies an approved draft over manually. The fail-safe guarantees no card is ever left in a P0–P3 state with no working next action. The PM is always the final authority.

---

## Forever takeaway

> Trust in an AI product is won or lost in the three seconds it takes to show your work — cite it, score it, and hand over the off switch, or the user quietly goes back to doing it by hand.
